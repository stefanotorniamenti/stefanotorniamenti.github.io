My research explores **how black holes form and grow**, and what their properties reveal about the star clusters and galaxies in which they originate. I connect theoretical and computational models to electromagnetic and gravitational-wave observations.

## Star cluster dynamics

The birth properties and dynamical evolution of star clusters strongly influence their stellar and black hole populations. I study how stellar evolution, binary interactions, and the initial structure of clusters shape their long-term evolution and observable properties.

## Black holes and gravitational waves

Dense stellar environments can produce and transform binary black holes through dynamical interactions and hierarchical mergers. I investigate their masses, spins, and merger rates, and their connection to gravitational-wave observations.

## Intermediate-mass black holes

I study whether dense stellar clusters can form intermediate-mass black holes through stellar collisions, hierarchical black hole mergers, and other growth pathways. A central question is whether these objects can contribute to the formation of massive black hole seeds.

## Galactic nuclei

Star clusters can migrate toward galactic centers and contribute to the assembly of nuclear star clusters. I investigate how this migration transports black holes into galactic nuclei and influences their subsequent growth.

## inSpyral

I developed **inSpyral**, a population-synthesis framework coupling star cluster formation, orbital migration, and black hole evolution within galaxies. The framework enables population-level studies of these connected processes across cosmic time.
